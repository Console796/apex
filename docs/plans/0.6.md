# Apex: 0.6 implementation plan: Lighting I: realtime preview

**Status (2026-10-29): 0.6.1 to 0.6.5 have landed on `master`, each with a progress note below. 0.6.6 to 0.6.8 and the 0.6 release have not started.** The decisions D3, D6/D8, D7/D13 and D16 were confirmed by the owner on 2026-10-29. The appendix records the
confirmed decisions. The tolerance stays provisional until it is calibrated against captures. 0.5 is not yet released (its remaining
gates are in [0.5.md](0.5.md)).

Working plan for the milestone [roadmap.md](../roadmap.md) describes as:

> **0.6: Lighting I: realtime preview.** A light model shared with the baker: `light`, `light_spot`,
> `light_environment`, and `light_rt` with direct/indirect/specular modes, using Source falloff formulas
> exactly. Clustered forward+ with a shadow atlas, sun and sky ambient, HDR and tone mapping matched to
> the game, fog, 2D and 3D skybox preview, better LightmappedGeneric/PBR/WorldVertexTransition fidelity,
> cubemap preview.
> **Exit:** direct lighting matches in-game captures within an agreed per-pixel tolerance on reference
> scenes, and `light_rt` dynamic lights match the game.

The design is [research.md §4](../research.md), tier 1. This document does not supersede research.md or
the roadmap; where they disagree, those win, and the 0.6 release lists the amendments. Two decisions
change what an earlier plan said:

- D1 adds a `lighting/` layer to the layer graph (0.1 §2; research §6 already names `lighting/`), with
  `core` and `document` below it and `render` above it.
- D11 re-confirms 0.1 D14 (GLSL, not Slang) at the point 0.1 named for reconsidering it.

0.6 also owes answers that earlier plans handed forward:

- **Light and clustered-light helpers** (0.4 D5, "lights waiting for 0.6"): D12.
- **Static prop lighting, `disableshadows` and fade previews** (0.4 §4): D7 and D12.
- **The `Lit` display mode** (0.1 D15's `DisplayMode = … | (Lit: 0.6)`): D4.
- **Whether GLSL still serves** once clustered lighting and PBR arrive (0.1 D14's reversal condition): D11.

**What 0.6 inherits.**

- **`viewport::DisplayMode::Lit`**, an enum stub that `ui/viewport_pane.cpp` maps to Textured today.
- **The renderer** (`render/renderer.cpp`): dynamic rendering, reversed-Z, a static pipeline table
  (`kSpecs`), one pipeline layout with a bindless texture heap (set 0) and 128 bytes of push constants,
  which are full. Buffer-device-address is enabled and already used (`preview.inc`). `cmake/Shaders.cmake`
  compiles `comp` stages.
- **`RenderMirror::buildDrawList`**, built incrementally from document change sets (0.1 principle 4): the
  place where light entities become per-frame light data.
- **Light editing** (`editor/light_edit.cpp`): handles for `_fifty_percent_distance`, `_zero_percent_distance`,
  `_distance`, `_hard_radius_override`, cones and `_constant_attn`, with ordering rules. **The spotlight pose
  and pitch convention** (`document::spotlightPose`), measured against native VRAD
  ([light_helpers.md](probe-results/light_helpers.md)).
- **FGD defaults written at compile time** (`editor::missingEntityDefaults`), because VRAD does not read the
  FGD ([lighting_compilation.md](probe-results/lighting_compilation.md)). The preview must apply the same
  defaults, or it shows a light the compiler darkens.
- **The real-tool compile path** (0.5): snapshots, native `vbsp`/`vrad`, `bsppp`. It produces the reference
  data D3 compares against.
- **Golden-image tests on lavapipe** (`tests/render/golden`, 8-bit, exact on llvmpipe).

**What 0.6 does not have.**

- **A light model.** Nothing computes attenuation, cones or sun light; keys are read as strings by the
  handles only: D1, D2.
- **An HDR target, tone mapping, a second pass of any kind** (shadows, tonemap): D5, D6.
- **Material inputs beyond `$basetexture`.** `assets::Material` has no `$bumpmap`, PBR textures, `$envmap`,
  `$selfillum` or blend modulation; HDR VTFs are clamped to [0,1]; the heap holds no float formats: D9.
- **Sky, fog and cubemaps**: D10.
- **A reference to compare against**, beyond eyeballing: D3.

## 1. Decisions

Each records what would reverse it.

### D1: The light model is a pure `lighting/` layer, built from entity keys once and consumed by render now and the baker in 0.7

`lighting/` depends on `core` and `document` (for `spotlightPose` and entity blocks) and nothing else; no
GPU, no Qt. It turns an entity's keys (after D2's defaults) into a `Light` value: kind, position, direction,
linear-space intensity per HDR/LDR, attenuation coefficients, effective radius, cone cosines and exponent,
sun spread, ambient, and `light_rt`'s per-channel modes. It also evaluates the model on the CPU
(`irradiance(light, point, normal)`), which the tests, the handles and 0.7's baker share. `render` gains it
as a dependency and uploads `Light` values; the GLSL evaluation is a transcription tested against the CPU
one (Bite 1).

FGD defaults have to be applied the same way for compile and preview (Bite 3). Today that function,
`missingEntityDefaults`, sits in `editor/entity_defaults.hpp`, which is above `lighting`. It moves down to
`document/entity_helpers.hpp`, next to `entityHelperValues`, and `editor` keeps a forwarding include.
The two stay distinct on purpose: helpers show read-only FGD defaults too (a camera frustum's far
distance), while the compiler, and so the light model, sees only what the snapshot writes. This move is the first change in 0.6.1, made before `lighting/`
exists.

The alternative is to keep the formulas in shaders, with the handles reading keys as now. It is less code
today and guarantees that the handles, the preview and 0.7's baker drift apart: three places would parse
`_light`, and a fix to one would not reach the others.

*What would reverse it:* a measured cost of the CPU model on large maps that the renderer cannot hide
(it runs once per light edit, so this is unlikely).

### D2: Formulas come from measuring native VRAD and the game, never from Valve SDK source

Every key-to-parameter rule (gamma of `_light`, brightness scaling, `_lightHDR`, the 50%/0% distance fit,
the 100-unit normalisation, cone cosines, sun records) is established by compiling probe maps with the
native tools and reading the worldlight lumps back, and is recorded in a probe document with the numbers
that prove it. Public documentation (the Valve Developer Community wiki) is cited where it agrees.
Strata-only behaviour (`light_rt` modes, worldlight v2) has no other source anyway.

The alternative, reading the leaked or SDK 2013 source, is faster and would be wrong for this project: the
SDK licence is incompatible with principle 6 (MIT now, GPLv3-or-later later), and code derived from it
could not be relicensed. Measurement is also what parity actually needs, because Strata has changed VRAD.

*What would reverse it:* Strata publishing its tools' source under a compatible licence.

### D3: Parity is three separate automated tests plus fixed-exposure game captures, not one VRAD comparison

The roadmap's exit compares with in-game captures. Those need the game running, so they cannot gate CI on
their own, and one end-to-end comparison would not say *where* a mismatch comes from. 0.6 separates the
question into tests that each answer one thing:

1. **Parameter parity (native).** Probe maps compiled by native VRAD; the worldlight lump read back and
   compared with the `Light` that D1 builds from the same keys. This checks the key→parameter rules (D2)
   exactly, to float precision, with no sampling in the way.
2. **Transcription parity (internal).** The GLSL light model evaluated at explicitly supplied positions and
   normals, written to a float buffer by a compute pass, and compared with the CPU model. This catches a
   shader that drifts from D1. It says nothing about native parity, and does not pretend to.
3. **Irradiance parity (native).** Native VRAD with `-bounce 0`, HDR explicitly compiled, compared with the
   CPU model on **flat, unbumped, unoccluded interior patches** only. Each compiled face fragment's lightmap
   coordinates, offsets, styles and HDR lump are reconstructed from the BSP (VBSP splits faces, so
   `hammerfaceid` names provenance, not a unique chart); LDR fallback is rejected; the lump's storage
   quantization is decoded before any percentage is computed. VRAD's sampling settings (supersampling,
   luxel offsets) are measured, not assumed, before the patch comparison is trusted.

The release judgment adds **in-game captures at a fixed camera and fixed exposure** (scripted launch, 0.5
D9, with `setpos`/`setang` and the tonemap controller pinned). They compare material response, tone
mapping and `light_rt`, which VRAD does not bake, against the editor's Lit render numerically on agreed
regions, with the owner's review for the rest.

The alternative, VRAD's lightmap as a pointwise oracle at every luxel centre, looks simpler but compares
two different sampling operators: supersampling, sample relocation near boundaries, smoothing groups and
bumped bases all differ from evaluating at a nominal centre, so its error budget would be spent on
sampling, not on the model. Gating on captures alone makes the tolerance subjective and hides the source
of a mismatch.

*What would reverse it:* test 3 showing VRAD's direct pass disagreeing with in-game direct light on the
same patches by more than the tolerance (0.6.2's P0 checks one scene), which would drop it in favour of
captures.

### D4: `Lit` is a display mode of the existing renderer, not a second renderer

`render::DisplayMode` gains `Lit`; `renderMode` maps `viewport::DisplayMode::Lit` to it. Lit draws the same
draw lists with lit pipelines (new `Kind`s), into an HDR target, followed by a tone-map pass. Wireframe,
Flat and Textured stay as they are and keep their goldens. Each viewport chooses its mode, so a lit
perspective view sits beside wireframe orthographic views (principle 1).

The alternative, a separate deferred renderer for Lit, gives cheaper many-light shading but duplicates
selection highlight, ghosts, previews and helpers, and makes translucent surfaces (glass, which mappers
light) a separate path. Forward+ keeps one path for all of them.

*What would reverse it:* forward shading proving too slow on lavapipe or integrated GPUs with the light
counts of real maps (0.6.6 measures this).

### D5: The renderer gains explicit passes with their own attachment signatures; lighting data is reached through one frame-block address

Today every pipeline is built for one renderer-wide colour/depth format pair (`renderer.cpp:342`), and
`record()` assumes a pass is already open (`renderer.hpp:216`). Lit needs at least three attachment
signatures: depth-only (shadow tiles), RGBA16F + depth (lit scene) and swapchain-format colour only
(tone map and overlays). 0.6.3 therefore starts by making passes explicit: each `Kind` in `kSpecs` names
the pass signature it is built for, a pass owns its attachments and their layout transitions, and the
heap gains render-target registration (images created with attachment usage, depth-aspect transitions,
lifetime tied to the viewport's size). This is the bulk of D5's work and the riskiest part of 0.6.3.

Per-frame data (lights, cluster lists, shadow matrices, exposure) lives in buffers reached through one
**frame block** whose device address is in the push constants. The push-constant ABI is fixed now: the
128 bytes keep their current layout for everything except `p1`, whose first 8 bytes (today the preview
address) become the frame-block address, and the preview address moves into the frame block. Mesh alpha
stays in the last word. The shadow atlas is a depth texture in the bindless heap, sampled through a
third immutable sampler (comparison, `GREATER_OR_EQUAL` because shadow tiles use reversed-Z like the
main view, Bite 9). Adding it changes
the heap's set layout once; that is accepted, since every pipeline is rebuilt with the pass work anyway.

The alternative is a second descriptor set for lighting. A set bound once per frame adds no per-draw
descriptor work either, so the honest argument for the frame block is smaller: it adds no second layout
to keep in step across every pipeline, and it reuses the BDA path `preview.inc` already proves.

*What would reverse it:* a target driver without buffer device address, or validation trouble with a
depth texture and a comparison sampler in the heap (0.6.3's P0 checks lavapipe, RADV and the Windows VM).

### D6: Lit renders to RGBA16F and tone-maps with a fixed exposure taken from the map, not auto-exposure

The HDR target is RGBA16F at the viewport's size. The tone-map pass applies exposure, the game's curve and
gamma, then writes the swapchain format. Exposure is fixed: from the map's `env_tonemap_controller`
(its default or a chosen min/max), else a profile default, with a per-viewport override. The curve is the
one 0.6.4's P0 measures from in-game captures of a known scene (Bite 4); until then it is a documented
placeholder, and goldens record it.

The alternative is the game's auto-exposure. It would match a moving in-game camera, but an editor view
whose brightness changes as you fly through it makes lights impossible to tune, and auto-exposure also
needs history across frames that offscreen goldens do not have.

*What would reverse it:* the owner preferring a game-like adapting view as an option (it can be added
later behind a toggle without changing the target).

### D7: Every light shadows in the preview, from brushes and props, within an atlas budget

Static lights are baked in the game, so their shadows are a lightmap's; the preview draws them with shadow
maps, which is what Hammer++ shows and what mappers expect. `light_spot` and `light_rt` spots get one atlas
tile; point lights six; the sun two or three cascades. Static props cast unless `disableshadows` is set.
Lights beyond the budget render unshadowed and are listed in the viewport's status line, never silently
dropped. How tiles are allocated, filtered and culled is D13.

The alternative, shadows only for `light_rt` (the game's dynamic lights), is cheaper and faithful to the
runtime path, but then a static light leaks through walls in the preview, which is the main thing a lighting
preview is for.

*What would reverse it:* atlas cost on real maps exceeding the frame budget at the default size even with
contribution culling.

### D8: Tier 1 shows direct light and the sun's ambient only; bounce and AO are 0.7's

Lit evaluates each light's direct term, the sun with its spread, and `light_environment`'s ambient as a
uniform hemisphere term. Nothing approximates bounce (no fake ambient fill per light). `light_rt` channels
set to *indirect* only contribute nothing in Lit and are flagged as "baked in 0.7" on their helper. The
preview is therefore darker than the game in enclosed spaces, by design and stated in the UI.

The alternative, a cheap ambient fill, looks closer to the game and would make D3's comparison meaningless
and mislead mappers about what bounce will do.

*What would reverse it:* the owner preferring a closer-looking preview before 0.7, as an explicit toggle.

### D9: Material fidelity covers the shaders mappers use, from VMT parameters, with brush tangents from texture axes

0.6 reads `$bumpmap`, `$basetexture2`/`$bumpmap2` with vertex alpha for WorldVertexTransition, `$blendmodulatetexture`,
`$selfillum`, `$envmap`/`$envmaptint`, VertexLitGeneric `$phong`, `Refract` with `$localrefract` (Portal 2's
light-strip covers, `models/props_lab/glass_lightcover*.mdl` → `glass/glasswindow_refract01*`, fake their
ribbed depth this way), and Strata PBR (`$mraotexture`, normal and, where cheap, parallax off). Brush tangents come from each face's texture S/T axes, which is how the engine builds them for
brushes; models use their own. HDR VTFs load as RGBA16F into the heap (the heap gains float formats).
Unknown shaders fall back to base texture only, listed once in the log, as 0.4 does for helpers.

The alternative, a PBR-only approximation for every material, is simpler and wrong for the bulk of P2CE
content, which is LightmappedGeneric.

*What would reverse it:* a measured mismatch showing a parameter the game ignores in a way the preview
cannot match cheaply.

**Amendment (0.6.5 a/b).** Reconstruct a cotangent frame per fragment from screen-space
position/UV derivatives, using the final mapped brush UVs (hence the face texture axes),
and use the same path for models. This avoids a vertex-format change and follows preview
transforms, rotated and mirrored UVs. Each projected texture gradient is normalized independently;
collapsed UVs retain the geometric normal. Model tangents are inferred from UVs rather than
loaded from the model: unusual authored tangent discontinuities independent of UV/normal seams
remain a preview limitation. Normal decoding and the measured +V green convention are recorded
in [material_normals_0_6_5.md](probe-results/material_normals_0_6_5.md).

### D10: Sky, fog and cubemaps are previews of the map's own entities

The 2D skybox draws the worldspawn `skyname` faces behind everything in Lit and Textured. The 3D skybox
draws the area around `sky_camera`, scaled by its `scale` about the camera, before the main scene, as the
game does. `env_fog_controller` drives distance fog in Lit. Cubemaps are rendered by the editor at each
`env_cubemap` from the Lit view (on demand and after edits settle), and surfaces with `$envmap env_cubemap`
use the nearest one, as `buildcubemaps` would assign it. Parallax correction is in scope only for
Strata's parallax-corrected cubemaps.

The alternative for cubemaps, reading them from a compiled BSP's pakfile, is exact for the last compile
but stale after any edit, and needs a compile before the first preview.

*What would reverse it:* editor-built cubemaps visibly differing from the game's in a way that confuses
mappers, which would add the BSP path as a comparison.

### D11: Shaders stay GLSL with includes

0.1 D14 said to revisit Slang when the lighting shader families arrive. They need shared functions (the light
model, the material inputs), not generics; `#include` with `common.inc`-style files covers that, and
`glslc` is already in the build. The light model's GLSL transcription is tested against D1's CPU version.

*What would reverse it:* 0.7's baker needing the same light model in a ray-query shader and a CPU path
with real code sharing that GLSL includes make painful.

### D12: Light helpers draw from the light model, so a handle, its sphere and the shading agree

`light`, `light_spot`, `light_environment`, `light_rt` and `clusteredlight` draw a sprite, a colour swatch from
the model's intensity, and the model's effective radius (sphere, or cone with inner and outer rims); the
sun draws its direction. The existing `light_edit.cpp` handles read the radius from the same model, so
dragging `_zero_percent_distance` moves the sphere and the light in one change. Static props draw lit in Lit
mode, respect `disableshadows`, and fade between `fademindist` and `fademaxdist` when the viewport's fade
preview is on.

*What would reverse it:* nothing expected; this pays 0.4's hand-forwards.

The model is built from the keys the viewport *shows*, including the temporary keys a live handle drag
publishes through `preview.setEntityKeys` (`light_edit.cpp:175`), not only from committed document
changes. Otherwise the light stays where it was until the mouse is released (Bite 8).

### D13: Shadow tiles are allocated stably with hysteresis, filtered with fixed-kernel PCF, and cast by what the viewport shows

- **Allocation.** A light keeps its tile and resolution until its screen contribution changes by more
  than a factor of two, or it leaves the frustum for more than a second. Ranking every frame from
  scratch is simpler, but it makes shadows pop and swap resolution as the camera moves, which reads as a
  bug while tuning a light.
- **Resolution and filtering.** Atlas 4096², tiles 256–1024 in powers of two; 3×3 PCF through the
  comparison sampler; slope-scaled depth bias plus a normal offset, both measured on the reference
  scenes. Point lights render six faces with a one-texel guard band, so the seams don't show.
- **Casters.** Hidden geometry (visgroups, the editor's hide) does not cast, so the preview matches what
  is drawn. Geometry that does not reach the compile (`func_detail` does, `nodraw` brushes do in VRAD)
  follows VRAD's rule, which 0.6.1's probe records. A toggle to let hidden geometry cast is not in 0.6.

*What would reverse it:* the hysteresis rules visibly lagging behind fast edits, or VRAD casting rules
that cannot be expressed per draw.

### D14: Clusters list every light whose finite range reaches them; unbounded lights get a cutoff, and overflow is reported

Clustering does not wait for shadows: a cluster entry is a light index, and the light record carries an
optional shadow tile. Range comes from the model (D1). Lights whose attenuation never reaches zero
(constant-only, or `_zero_percent_distance` unset with a quadratic term) are cut off where their
contribution falls below 1/256 of the frame's exposure-adjusted white, the same threshold the helper
sphere draws (D12). The sun and ambient are global, not clustered. Each cluster holds up to 64 lights. Any
overflow is clamped and counted, and the count is shown in the status line beside the shadow budget.

*What would reverse it:* real maps routinely overflowing 64 per cluster at the default grid, which would
grow the grid before it grows the list.

### D15: Editor overlays draw after tone mapping, in display space

Selection highlight, wireframe overlays, helpers, gizmos and the grid are drawn after the tone-map pass,
in the swapchain format, exactly as they look in Textured mode. Drawing them in the HDR target would make
their colours change with exposure. A light helper's colour swatch is the one exception: it shows the
light's tone-mapped colour, which is the useful answer to "what colour is this light".

*What would reverse it:* overlays needing depth-correct blending with lit translucency that a post-tonemap
pass cannot give.

### D16: Sky ambient applies only where the sky is visible; editor cubemaps are rebuilt explicitly and never see themselves

- **Sky ambient.** `light_environment`'s ambient term is gated by a coarse sky-visibility estimate: a
  low-resolution sky-occlusion probe grid computed on the CPU from brush geometry after edits settle.
  Without it a sealed room glows with sky light that the game's bake would never give it. That would be the
  most visible lie in the preview.
- **Cubemaps.** An `env_cubemap` is rendered from the Lit view with every cubemap-reflective material
  sampling a neutral fallback, so a cubemap never reflects itself or its neighbours (the game's
  `buildcubemaps` does two passes; one is enough for a preview). Rebuilding is on demand, plus
  automatically for cubemaps whose 1,024-unit neighbourhood changed, once edits have settled for a second.
  A stale cubemap is drawn with a badge on its helper.

*What would reverse it:* the sky-visibility grid costing more than a frame on the largest corpus map (it
would move to the GPU), or the owner preferring the game's two-pass look.

## 2. Where the seam goes

```
  ui/            Lit in the mode menu; exposure control; cubemap rebuild command; status of shadow budget
       |
  viewport/      maps DisplayMode::Lit; per-viewport exposure override
       |
  render/        lit pipelines, HDR target, tone map, shadow atlas, clusters (compute), sky/fog/cubemap passes;
       |         uploads lighting::Light values; GLSL light model (tested against lighting/)
  lighting/      NEW. Light from keys + defaults, the CPU light model, sun/ambient, light_rt modes. Pure.
       |
  document/      entity blocks, spotlightPose (unchanged)
  assets/        VMT parameters (D9), float VTFs, sky textures
  core/          unchanged
```

`editor/` gains `lighting` as a dependency for the handles (D12). `tools/check_layers.py` and CMake's
`apex_layer` change together.

## 3. Sub-milestones

Reference validation comes first (0.6.1–0.6.2): it decides whether the model is right before any
renderer work depends on it. The renderer then lands in small, separately testable steps.

### 0.6.1: The light model

**P0.** The worldlight probe (D2): native VRAD's conversion of every light key into the worldlight lumps,
LDR and HDR, including `light_rt`; layout of worldlight v2; which brushes cast (D13). `tools/light_probe.py`,
[lighting_0_6_p0.md](probe-results/lighting_0_6_p0.md).
**Work.** Move `missingEntityDefaults` into `document` (D1). `lighting/` layer; `Light` from keys with the
defaults; CPU irradiance; finite range and cutoff (D14); handles read radii from it (D12's handle part).
**Tests.** D3 test 1: every probe row reproduced to float precision. A light with missing keys equals one
with the FGD defaults written. Layer check.

**Progress (2026-10-29).** P0 done: [lighting_0_6_p0.md](probe-results/lighting_0_6_p0.md) settles every key
rule, including the 50%/0% solve, which needed a dense 343-ratio sweep (an iterated constant term, accepted
once the curve does not brighten one world unit out). Only the HDR worldlight lump is written. The
defaults move and `lighting/light.{hpp,cpp}` are in. `tests/lighting/light_test.cpp` checks D3 test 1
against all 460 probe cases (464 worldlights, `tests/fixtures/lighting/worldlights.json`, regenerated with
`tools/light_probe.py --fixture`). The handles need no change: they edit the keys whose values are the
radii, and their ordering rule (zero pushed past fifty) already avoids VRAD's z = f special case. The
model's `effectiveRange` for lights without 50%/0% keys moves to 0.6.4 with the helpers (D12), which is
also where `render` and `editor` first take the `lighting` dependency. The cone and ambient shapes of
`irradiance()` are provisional until 0.6.2 measures them.

### 0.6.2: The irradiance reference

**P0.** VRAD's sampling settings and HDR lump encoding, measured on a one-light probe map. One scene compiled
`-bounce 0` and captured in the game at fixed exposure, to check that VRAD's direct pass is a valid
reference (D3's reversal).
**Work.** `tools/light_parity.py`: fragment reconstruction (lightmap coordinates, offsets, styles), HDR-only
decoding, flat-unbumped-unoccluded patch selection, comparison with the CPU model, per-scene statistics
(appendix). Scripted fixed-exposure captures.
**Tests.** D3 test 3 as a gated stage over the reference scenes, with the provisional tolerance.
**Progress (2026-10-29).** Harness and measurements done:
[lighting_0_6_2_reference.md](probe-results/lighting_0_6_2_reference.md). `tools/light_parity.py` compiles
eight scenes with `-bounce 0 -noextra -aoscale 0` and passes every one (worst mean 0.58%, worst p99 2.6%,
on sky ambient). The measured model replaces Lambert with (c + c²)/2, adds the spot's axis cosine, and gives
sky ambient a closed form. The 0.6.1 model fails the gate (+19–34%). The default compile bakes ambient
occlusion, which the model omits. Still open: the in-game `-bounce 0` capture (needs the game running),
the second lightmap per style, and sky ambient in the C++ fixture (needs visibility).

### 0.6.3: Passes and HDR targets

**P0.** A depth texture and a comparison sampler in the heap, and a float storage buffer read back from a
compute pass, validation-clean on lavapipe, RADV and the Windows VM.
**Work.** Explicit passes with attachment signatures and heap render-target registration (D5).
RGBA16F target and tone-map pass with a placeholder curve (D6). Overlays
after tone mapping (D15). `DisplayMode::Lit` still draws Textured shading, now through the new passes.
**Tests.** Every existing golden unchanged (Textured, Flat and Wireframe go through the new passes).
A compute readback round-trips known floats.
**Progress.** SceneTarget owns viewport-sized RGBA16F/D32 attachments registered separately from uploads.
Lit meshes use scene-signature pipelines; a fullscreen clamp/exposure pass restores colour and depth
before display-space helpers and overlays. The heap includes the reversed-Z comparison sampler.
The frame block and push-constant ABI change moved to 0.6.4; the existing preview ABI is preserved. After
review: selection and hover tints draw after the tone map, depth-tested against the copied scene depth.
Resize reuses the old heap slots, so a heap with room for one target can still resize it. Every frame
transitions the scene target from UNDEFINED. The comparison probe covers equal depths.

### 0.6.4: Lit, unshadowed

**P0.** Capture one known scene in the game at a fixed camera and exposure; fit the tone-map curve (Bite 4).
**Work.** Frame block and new push-constant ABI (D5); forward loop over all lights; sun and ambient (D8);
sky-visibility gate (D16); light helpers and live-drag keys (D12).
**Tests.** D3 test 2: GLSL vs CPU at supplied points into a float buffer. Goldens for a box room with
one light of each kind.

**Progress (2026-10-29, core slice).** The 128-byte push ABI now carries a frame-block address,
including preview transforms and the sprite camera basis. Per-slot uploads rebase double world-space
lights to the camera. Lit evaluates the measured point/spot/sun model in a forward loop, with no fill;
`fullbright` provides unit irradiance. UNORM albedo is explicitly 2.2-decoded; the placeholder tone map
applies exposure, clamps and 2.2-encodes for UNORM display targets. Helpers, ghosts, overlays and tints
remain display-space. The mirror caches lights from class defaults plus live keys/transforms, resolves
named targets (first visible match), and includes instance lights. All styles currently stay at full

**Progress (sky visibility).** `lighting/sky_visibility.{hpp,cpp}`: a CPU probe grid over the BVH. Probes
default to 128-unit spacing, coarsened to fit a 32,768-probe cap, with a fixed stratified ray set. Each probe

skipped. A 4096³ room of ~300 triangles builds in about 2 s single-threaded at 256 rays. Not yet compared
with VRAD on a partly covered sky.

**Every formula below is provisional until fitted to the owner's fixed-exposure game
captures.** CPU references live in `lighting/specular.hpp`; GLSL is a transcription
checked by float compute probes, independently of render regression images.

- **MRAO:** `clamp(texture.rgb * $mraoscale, 0, 1)`; absent texture is `(0,1,1)`.
  Depends on `$mraotexture` RGB and `$mraoscale` (default `(1,1,1)`). Alpha does not
  affect opacity, AO or this step's specular. `$model` is resolved metadata; brush/model
  selection follows the existing mesh path and does not alter these formulas.
- **Diffuse:** existing linear albedo times measured direct irradiance times `(1-M)`.
  The existing `(c+c²)/2` response and intensity units stay intact: no new `1/pi` scale.
  This preserves dielectric diffuse exactly when specular is zero. Ambient additionally
  multiplies by AO; direct light never does. Depends on base texture/encoding, draw
  colour, normal/bump, MRAO R/B and scale, light parameters, sky grid and exposure.
- **GGX:** Cook-Torrance `D*F*G/(4*N.L*N.V)`, with `alpha=r²`,
  `D=alpha²/(pi*((N.H)²*(alpha²-1)+1)²)`, Schlick-GGX
  `G1(c)=c/(c*(1-k)+k)`, `k=(r+1)²/8`, and
  `F=F0+(1-F0)*(1-V.H)^5`, `F0=mix(0.04,albedo,M)`.
  Roughness is clamped to `[0.04,1]` to approximate the delta lobe finitely; nonpositive
  N.L or N.V gives zero. Depends on MRAO R/G and scale, linear albedo, normal/bump,
  light direction and camera. Public reference: Brian Karis,
  [Real Shading in Unreal Engine 4, SIGGRAPH 2013](https://cdn2.unrealengine.com/Resources/files/2013SiggraphPresentationsNotes-26915738.pdf),
  eqs. 2–4 and 8 (ordinary fifth-power Schlick Fresnel).
- **Phong:** an unnormalised **Blinn-Phong** half-vector lobe
  `max(N.H,0)^max(exponent,1) * max(boost,0) * max(tint,0) * mask * Fresnel`.
  Exponent is `$phongexponent` (default 5), overridden by exponent-texture R `*149+1`.
  The Fresnel curve is piecewise linear over `1-N.V`, through `$phongfresnelranges`
  at 0, 0.5 and 1 (default `(0,0.5,1)`). Tint defaults to white, boost to 1. The mask
  is bump alpha (1 without a bump), or base alpha with `$basemapalphaphongmask`.
  Depends on `$phong`, those five Phong parameters, both mask sources/selector,
  normal/bump, camera and light direction. Public parameter reference:
  [VDC $phong](https://developer.valvesoftware.com/wiki/$phong); the page returned 403
  during implementation, so the exponent mapping and curve contract follow the owner's
  supplied public-documentation semantics. Lobe choice, interpolation and normalisation
  especially need capture fitting.
- **Direct integration:** `incomingRadiance * BRDF * max(N.L,0)`, implemented as
  `measuredIrradiance * BRDF * 2/(1+N.L)` above the horizon, since measured irradiance
  already contains `(c+c²)/2`. Depends on each point/spot/sun's existing intensity,
  attenuation, radius, cone and direct-mode gate; it introduces no extra light scale.
  Sky ambient adds no specular. The loop marks the hook for separate `light_rt`
  direct/specular modes in 0.6.6; those modes are not implemented here.

**Design (0.6.5 d), 2026-10-29.** The public Valve and Strata wikis refuse automated fetches,
so each mask convention below is provisional and gets measured against the owner's captures
before it is fixed (D2). The test map uses only `PBR` with `$envmap env_cubemap`, so the PBR path
is fitted first.

- **Cube textures in the heap.** The heap gains binding 2, `textureCube cubes[]` (256 slots,
  partially bound, update-after-bind), with its own free list. Cube slot 0 is
  `engine/defaultcubemap`, which is what the game samples when no cubemaps are built. If that
  file cannot load, slot 0 is a mid-grey 1×1 cube instead. VTF cube faces (including RGBA16F HDR)
  upload with their mip chains. Vulkan's seamless cube filtering is on by default.
- **Probes.** The draw list carries `cubemaps` (world position, cube slot) for every
  `env_cubemap`, both the map's own and those inside instances, after transforms. The frame block
  points to them. A material whose `$envmap` is `env_cubemap` (case-insensitive) samples the probe
  nearest to the fragment. Known limitation: VBSP assigns one cubemap per brush side (the
  compiled test1 patch VMTs confirm it), so a face that spans two probes can show a seam here but
  not in the game. Per-side assignment comes with editor-built cubemaps in 0.6.8.
- **Probe textures.** `RenderMirror` takes an optional source that returns a cube texture for a
  probe origin. Without one, every probe uses cube slot 0. 0.6.8 supplies editor-built cubemaps
  through this hook. For fitting, `apex_render_pose --cubemaps DIR` loads
  `c<x>_<y>_<z>.hdr.vtf` (falling back to `.vtf`), named by the rounded origin as VBSP names them.
  DIR holds files extracted read-only from a compiled BSP's pakfile, so the comparison uses the
  game's own reflection input.
- **Named envmaps.** Any other `$envmap` value is a VTF loaded through the material cache into a
  cube slot owned by the material.
- **Shading (provisional, textbook only).** The reflection direction is `reflect(-V, N)` on the
  bumped normal.
  - *LightmappedGeneric, VertexLitGeneric and WorldVertexTransition:* `env = cube(R) * $envmaptint * mask`,
    added after lighting and not lit. The mask is `$envmapmask` RGB, bump alpha with
    `$normalmapalphaenvmapmask`, or base alpha with `$basealphaenvmapmask`, read directly and
    not inverted (unverified). With no mask source the mask is 1.
  - *PBR:* `env = cube(R, lod = roughness * maxLod) * EnvBRDF(F0, roughness, N·V) * AO * $envmaptint`,
    where EnvBRDF is Karis's published analytic fit (*Physically Based Shading on Mobile*, 2014),
    which avoids a lookup texture. `$envmaplightscale` (default 0) blends the reflection toward
    lighting-scaled: `env *= mix(1, luminance(diffuse irradiance), $envmaplightscale)`. Its
    meaning is inferred from the name and is the first thing the captures must confirm.
  - *Fullbright* keeps the envmap term, as the game's captures show.
  - *`$selfillum`:* `base.rgb * $selfillumtint * mask` is added unlit. The mask is base alpha,
    or `$selfillummask` R when that is present.
- **Record.** The material record grows to 128 bytes: envmap cube slot, envmap mask slot,
  self-illumination mask slot, flags (ENVMAP, ENVMAP_PROBE, BASE_ALPHA_ENVMAP_MASK,
  NORMAL_ALPHA_ENVMAP_MASK, SELFILLUM), `envmapTint` (w holds `$envmaplightscale`) and
  `selfIllumTint`. Offsets are statically checked as before.
- **Deferred.** `$envmapcontrast`, `$envmapsaturation`, `$envmapfresnel`, `$envmapmasktransform`,
  `$selfillumfresnel` and parallax-corrected cubemaps (0.6.8).

## 5. Things that will bite

**1. The GLSL light model drifts from the CPU one.** A shader tweak to make a golden look right silently
diverges from the baker's model. *Defence:* a test renders probe points and compares the HDR target with D1's
CPU result; goldens do not count as model tests.

**2. `_light` is gamma-encoded and `A` is brightness.** Treating `R G B` as linear, or `A` as a 0–1 scale, is
the obvious mistake. *Defence:* D2's probe rows are the unit tests.

**3. Missing keys mean FGD defaults, not zero.** VRAD reads no FGD; Apex writes defaults at compile time.
*Defence:* the model is built from keys after `missingEntityDefaults`, one function for both paths, moved
into `document` so that `lighting` can call it (D1).

**4. Tone mapping hides model errors.** A wrong curve can be "fixed" by tuning light formulas.
*Defence:* D3 compares in linear HDR before tone mapping; the curve is fitted separately from captures.

**5. Pitch sign.** Source light pitch is elevation, positive up, and `light_environment`'s `pitch` key
overrides the angles. *Defence:* `spotlightPose` is the only conversion; the sun reuses it.

**6. Lavapipe is slow at shadow passes.** Goldens with many lights time out in CI. *Defence:* golden scenes
keep one to three lights; performance is measured on the reference host, not asserted on lavapipe.

**7. 8-bit goldens cannot carry parity.** *Defence:* parity is D3's float comparisons; goldens only guard
regressions in the image.

**8. Lighting rebuilt only on document changes goes stale during a drag.** Handle drags publish temporary
keys through `preview.setEntityKeys` (`light_edit.cpp:175`) and commit only on release. A mirror that
listens only to change sets shows the old light for the whole drag. *Defence:* the light list is built from
the preview's effective keys, and a UI test drags `_zero_percent_distance` and samples the Lit target
before release.

**9. Reversed-Z flips the shadow comparison.** A `LESS` comparison sampler copied from a textbook makes
every shadow inverted, or makes the lit side fully shadowed. *Defence:* shadow tiles use the main view's
depth convention, and 0.6.7(a)'s first golden is a single pillar.

**10. Exclusion masks hide broken shadows.** D3 test 3 drops occluded and boundary luxels, so a shadow that
leaks or is displaced would pass. *Defence:* every report states the masked fraction, and the shadow
goldens and the leak test are separate gates (0.6.7).

**11. The heap assumes sampled colour images.** Uploads create sampled/transfer-destination images with
colour-aspect transitions (`texture_heap.cpp:206`), so registering a depth attachment fails or validates
badly. *Defence:* render-target registration is its own heap entry point with its own tests (0.6.3).

- **D7/D13: every light shadows within a budget; hidden geometry does not cast.** Turned down: shadows for
  `light_rt` only (static lights would leak through walls), and casting from hidden geometry. Reopen if atlas
  cost exceeds the frame budget on real maps.
- **D16: sky ambient gated by sky visibility.** Turned down: ungated ambient (sealed rooms glow), and no
  ambient at all (outdoors much too dark). Reopen if the visibility grid costs more than a frame on the
  largest corpus map.
- **The tolerance**, provisional until 0.6.2's P0 calibrates it. It is measured per reference scene in linear
  HDR irradiance (VRAD's units after decoding), on luminance (Rec. 709 weights) with per-channel error
  reported alongside. Error per sample is `|model − ref| / max(ref, floor)`, where the floor is 1% of the
  scene's 99th-percentile reference luminance, so dark samples are judged absolutely, not as a ratio.
  Proposed targets are mean under 2% and 99th percentile under 6%. Samples come only from flat, unbumped,
  unoccluded patches whose reference-derived mask excludes luxels within one luxel of a shadow or face
  edge. Every report states the excluded fraction, and shadow leaks are tested separately (Bite 10).
