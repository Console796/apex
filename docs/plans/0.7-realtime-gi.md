# 0.7 exploration: realtime fake global illumination

**Status: exploration, not a decision.** Written 2026-11-01 against 0.6.7 (shadowed spot, point and sun lights in Lit).

## 1. The problem

Lit is a clustered forward+ renderer with a shadow atlas. Everything a surface receives is either a direct
light (shadowed or not) or the sky-ambient term, which is `light_environment` ambient scaled by the CPU sky
probe grid (`sampleSkyVisibility`, `lighting.inc`). In a sealed room that term is 0 by design, because
VRAD's bake gives sealed rooms no sky ambient either. So any surface a light cannot see directly is black,
such as the ceiling under the overhang in the report. VRAD's bounce (`-final` radiosity over patches, using VTF
reflectivity) would put some light there; Lit has no equivalent.

What exists to build on:

| Have | Where |
|---|---|
| HDR scene colour + reversed-Z D32 depth, both sampleable | `scene_target.hpp` |
| Clustered lights, shadow atlas, light `indirectMode` field | `gpu_lighting.hpp`, `clusters.hpp`, `shadows.hpp` |
| Coarse CPU probe grid (ambient cube per probe, trilinear + normal-weighted) | `sky_visibility.*` |
| Cubemap probes | `FrameBlock::cubemaps` |
| Vulkan 1.3, compute queue; `VK_KHR_ray_query` / `acceleration_structure` detected but not enabled | `context.cpp:265` |
| Planned for 0.7 anyway: ray-query GPU baker, Embree fallback, BVH over map geometry | `roadmap.md` |

What does not exist: a normal buffer (forward shading only), a depth prepass for the scene, motion vectors,
or any temporal history.

## 2. What "right" means here

Apex previews a *VRAD* result. VRAD bounce is diffuse, view-independent, and computed over the whole map.
That favours view-independent methods (probes, lightmaps, surfels) over screen-space ones for matching,
and it means a method that changes with camera angle will look "wrong" next to an A/B compare against the
bake. Realtime GI here is a **stand-in for the bake while editing**, not a replacement for it. The 0.7 exit
criterion (error vs VRAD under a threshold, re-convergence after moving a light) is measured on the baked
view; this note is about what the *unbaked* Lit view shows meanwhile.

## 3. Options

### A. Ambient floor with AO (baseline, days)
GTAO/XeGTAO (P2CE itself ships XeGTAO as runtime AO) from depth plus reconstructed normals, multiplied into
ambient and a small constant "fill" term tinted by a per-surface average reflectivity.
- Pro: cheap, no new data structures, matches the game's own AO.
- Con: AO only *darkens*. It cannot add light to the ceiling; it just stops fill from being flat.
- Verdict: do it anyway, because every other option wants AO to hide its low resolution.

### B. SSGI (screen-space radiance gather)
Per pixel, ray-march the depth buffer (HBIL / "horizon-based indirect lighting", or a short hierarchical march)
and gather the already-lit HDR colour of what the ray hits, weighted by cosine and BRDF, accumulated over
frames with a history buffer.
- Needs: normal + depth prepass (or G-buffer normals), previous-frame colour, motion vectors or reprojection
  via depth, a temporal + spatial denoiser, half-res gather.
- Cost: roughly 1 to 2 ms at 1080p half-res on mid GPUs; pure compute, no RT hardware needed.
- Pro: no precomputation, reacts instantly to light edits, works on any map size and with brush edits mid-drag.
- Con, and they matter for this editor:
  1. **Only sees what is on screen.** The report's ceiling is lit mainly by surfaces behind or below the camera;
     the visible neighbours are near-black panels (low albedo), so the gather returns little. SSGI would soften
     the strip next to the lit ceiling tile and not much else.
  2. **View-dependent.** Orbiting changes the result, which defeats A/B against a baked view and fights
     screenshot goldens (`tests/` has per-pixel golden captures).
  3. **Light leaking and disocclusion trails** while the camera flies, which is all an editor does.
  4. Forward renderer: adds a depth+normal prepass, which also costs the whole scene a second vertex pass.
- Verdict: helps contact bounce and colour bleeding at close range. Does not fix the stated problem. Reasonable as an
  optional layer *on top of* a world-space method, not as the primary.

### C. Irradiance probe volume, ray-traced and updated live (DDGI-style) — **recommended core**
A 3D grid of probes. Each frame a budgeted subset shoots N rays against the scene BVH, shades the hit with
direct light (shadow-map lookup or a second ray) plus the previous frame's probe irradiance (this gives
multi-bounce for free), and blends into an octahedral irradiance + depth (visibility) texture per probe.
Surface shading samples the 8 surrounding probes with Chebyshev visibility weights to avoid leaks.
- Matches VRAD's character: diffuse, view-independent, whole-map, and reflectivity-tinted.
- Sees offscreen geometry, so the ceiling gets light from the floor and lit walls no matter where the camera is.
- **Shares the 0.7 scene BVH and ray-query plumbing with the baker.** The incremental cost is the probe update
  compute shader and a sampler in `lighting.inc`, not a second geometry pipeline.
- Replaces the CPU `SkyVisibilityGrid` role: sky becomes just another emitter, hit-or-miss on the BVH. The grid
  (`skyOrigin`, `skyDims`, `skyCellSize`) is already in `FrameBlock`; the layout generalises.
- Map scale: coordinates go to ±65536. Use a camera-centred clipmap of 2 to 3 nested grids (e.g. 64, 256, 1024
  unit spacing) so cost is bounded and detail is near the camera.
- Cons: needs ray-query hardware (fallback: B, or A with fill only; Embree is CPU and too slow for live
  updates on big maps). Probe spacing limits detail (corners, thin walls need visibility terms and a
  normal-offset bias). Brush edits invalidate BVH regions: rebuild/refit cost on every drag; mitigation is to
  keep a dynamic TLAS with per-brush BLAS and refit only touched ones. Update lag of a few frames after a light moves.
- Verdict: best fit to the stated goal and to the 0.7 architecture.

### D. Voxel cone tracing / SDF / radiance cascades
Voxelise radiance into a 3D clip-map and cone-trace, or march an SDF (Lumen-like).
- Needs voxelisation of every brush each edit; planar brushes at 4 to 8 unit detail over 131072-unit maps need
  clip-maps and an injection pass; thin walls leak; cone tracing is expensive per pixel.
- Pro: no RT hardware. Con: more engine than C for worse visibility, and the baker still needs its own BVH, so no sharing.
- Verdict: rejected unless RT-less hardware must be supported as first class.

### E. Reflective shadow maps / light propagation volumes
RSM reuses the shadow pass (add flux + normal targets) and gathers one bounce per shadowed light; LPV injects and
floods SH in a grid.
- Cheap and RT-free but no occlusion for the bounce, one bounce only (RSM), heavy leaking and low frequency (LPV).
- Verdict: LPV/RSM quality is below what C gives and below what an editor user will accept on hard-edged architecture.

### F. Cheap fakes
- **Hemispherical fill light per light:** each shadowed light adds a low-intensity, unshadowed, wide wrap term
  weighted by distance to the light and by the reflectivity of the surface that light mostly hits. Hits the
  ceiling in the report for ~zero cost, but it leaks through walls (so gate it by the sky-grid style visibility
  or by cluster/BSP-leaf connectivity). Good only as the *no-RT fallback*.
- **Per-leaf average radiosity:** use PVS/portal data from a past compile; unavailable before the first compile.
- **Virtual point lights from RSM hits** (instant-radiosity): ~hundreds of tiny clustered lights; fits the existing
  cluster binning but needs the shadow cost per VPL (or none, accepting leaks).

## 4. Recommendation

1. **0.6.9 or 0.7.0 (small, independent): option A** — XeGTAO plus an `ambient_fill` floor with per-material
   reflectivity tint. Introduces the depth+normal prepass that B and the probe visibility terms use. Cheap,
   fixes "pure black".
2. **0.7.x (main): option C** — probe volume updated by ray queries on the baker's BVH, one cascade first,
   sky as an emitter, reflectivity tint from the material table. A/B against the baked view and against VRAD
   captures reuses the existing per-pixel tolerance harness.
3. **Later, optional: option B** half-res as a close-range detail layer on top of C, only if probes look too
   smooth in review. Default off.
4. **No-RT fallback:** F gated by the CPU probe grid's visibility, with a visible note in the viewport HUD.

## 5. Open questions / things to measure before committing

- Do target GPUs (owner's machine, CI Windows box) expose `VK_KHR_ray_query`? Enabling it is a context change; the
  flags are already reported at `context.cpp:265`.
- How large does the TLAS get on the reference maps and what is a per-drag refit time? This sets whether C is
  "live while dragging" or "converges after release".
- Probe leak behaviour on thin Source brushes (16-unit walls) at 64-unit spacing; may need a minimum-spacing
  clip-level or per-probe wall-offset relocation.
- How closely does one realtime bounce with reflectivity match `vrad -final` on the reference scenes? Cheap to
  test now: render the report scene with A + a placeholder constant fill and diff against a VRAD capture.
- Decide how the unbaked view labels itself: realtime GI is an approximation, and the compare view (0.7) should say which one it is showing.
- Interaction with `light_rt` `indirectMode` (None / Static / Dynamic): "Dynamic" lights should feed the probes
  and "Static" ones should not, mirroring the game.
