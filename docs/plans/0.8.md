# Apex: 0.8 implementation plan: Advanced geometry & surfaces

**Status (2026-11-10): reviewed by the owner; ready for 0.8.1.** Drafted 2026-11-10 against `0.7.7-release`
plus the post-release commits on `master` up to `a38fe4c`, which the owner numbered 0.7.8 (see the appendix).
No sub-milestone has started. Every decision was confirmed as drafted; the appendix records the alternatives
turned down. D7 stays gated on its P0 probe.

Working plan for the milestone [roadmap.md](../roadmap.md) describes as:

> **0.8: Advanced geometry & surfaces.** Displacements (create, sculpt, paint alpha/multiblend, sew, subdivide,
> and what each topology tool does to a displaced solid: clip, vertex, carve, hollow and merge refuse them until
> then, 0.3 D11), overlays (`info_overlay`, face sets), hotspot texturing (VTF hotspot resource + rect maps),
> decals, rope and cable preview, detail/structural tooling.
> **Exit:** organic and outdoor geometry workflows reach parity with Hammer++.

The design is [research.md](../research.md) §2 item 2 ("Displacements are their own subsystem: power 2–4,
sewing, normals/offsets, alpha, multiblend, sculpt brushes. Overlays: basis, UV points, face lists") and the
§1 Textures row (hotspot rects in the VTF or a rect map). This document does not supersede research.md or the
roadmap; where they disagree, those win, and the 0.8 release lists the amendments. One decision changes what
an earlier plan said:

- D5 replaces 0.3 D11 ("solids with displacement sides are read-only for topology tools until 0.8") with a rule
  per tool.

0.8 also owes answers that earlier plans handed forward:

- **`dispinfo` parsing and rendering** (0.1 §4: "preserved opaquely, and displacement faces render as their
  base face for now"): D1, D2.
- **What each topology tool does to a displaced solid** (0.3 D11): D5.
- **Hotspot texturing** (0.3 §4: "the face panel has no hotspot button"): D13.
- **Decals and overlays, including their `sides` references** (0.3 §4; 0.1 D21: "faces that disappear are
  reported in the ChangeSet, so overlays referencing them can be flagged (0.8)"): D10, D11.
- **The FGD `decal`, `overlay` and `overlay_transition` helpers** (0.4 D5 table: deferred "to 0.8, with
  overlays"): D10, D12.
- **WorldVertexTransition, `$blendmodulatetexture` and PBR blend layers** (0.6.5: not started, because only
  displacements carry per-vertex alpha): D8.
- **Overlays and decals lit** (0.6 §4): D10, D12.
- **Displacement lightmaps** (0.7 §3): D9.
- **Polygon tool, sprinkle, physics drop** (0.3 §4: "candidates for 0.8 or later"): not in 0.8 (§4, owner,
  2026-11-10).

**What 0.8 inherits.**

- **Solids stored as VMF blocks with derived geometry** (0.2 D4), sides by value in source order, and
  `SideId` as a face's identity (0.1 D21). A side's `dispinfo` already survives load and save
  (`vmf.hpp:118`, `OpaqueBlock dispInfo`), and an unmodified map saves byte-identical.
- **Face matching on rebuild** (`geometry::matchFaces`, 0.3 D2–D3), and the ChangeSet's `vanishedSides`
  (`document.hpp:68`). Nothing reads `vanishedSides` yet.
- **A correct displacement transform, in one place only.** Instance collapse already moves `startposition`
  and rotates `normals`, `offsets` and `offset_normals` (`instance_collapse.cpp:90`). D4 makes that function
  the only one.
- **Sidelist remapping, in one place only.** Instance collapse remaps FGD `sidelist` keys through the pasted
  side-ID map (`instance_collapse_command.cpp:98`). Ordinary paste and duplicate record the same map
  (`clipboard.cpp:583`, `group_ops.hpp:32`) and then drop it.
- **The tool interface** (0.3 D1): input, preview, overlay, readout and cancel behind `editor::Tool`, writing
  only through `EditorSession::edit()`; tool previews (0.3 D12); world overlay primitives and labels (0.3 D16).
- **Model-triangle picking** (0.4) and the scene BVH (0.7 D3), which displacement picking and the baker reuse.
- **The material pipeline** (0.6): VMT parameters, the bindless material table, and the Realtime shaders that
  WVT blending extends.
- **The baker, the VRAD round-trip and Hybrid** (0.7 D2–D9), all of which currently skip displacement sides.
- **The helper registry** (0.4 D5): `OverlayHelper`, `OverlayTransitionHelper` and `sidelist` parse; only
  `sidelist` draws (it highlights the listed faces).
- **sourcepp:** vtfpp reads the VTF hotspot resource (`vtfpp::HOT`, resource type 43). bsppp exposes
  `DISPINFO`, `DISP_VERTS`, `DISP_TRIS` and `DISP_LIGHTMAP_SAMPLE_POSITIONS`, which are the compiled oracle for
  D2 and D9.

**What 0.8 does not have.**

- **Any test data with a displacement.** None of the 512 P2CE corpus maps has a `dispinfo` side (0.3 D11 P0),
  and no `.vmf` anywhere in the installed Steam library has one either (checked 2026-11-10). On the same
  install 25 corpus maps have an `info_overlay`, 4 have a rope and 2 have an `infodecal`. No loose
  `.rect` file or VMT hotspot reference was found; VPK contents are unchecked. D3 addresses this.
  *Update (2026-11-10):* the owner added one power-3 displacement to `~/maps/apex/test1.vmf` (solid 637, side
  355). It is the first oracle case for D2, ahead of the full D3 set. Its `alphas` rows were written as the grid
  columns' X coordinates (`-96 -72 … 72 0`), not 0–255, after an alpha-painting attempt in Strata Hammer under
  Wine. The material, `nature/dirtfloor004c`, is plain `LightMappedGeneric` with no `$basetexture2`, so painting
  could not show anything there. Why Hammer wrote coordinates as alpha is unknown. Apex preserves such values
  byte-for-byte, clamps them for display, and flags them (D1).
- **A correct transform of displacement solids.** `editor::applyToSolid` (`transform.cpp:163`) moves the base
  face but never touches `dispinfo`. Since 0.3 D11 says transforms "keep working on them", a moved, rotated or
  mirrored displacement is already saved with a stale `startposition` and unrotated normals. Rotating a
  sculpted hill by 90° and saving gives a hill that sculpts sideways in Hammer and in the compile.
  This bug is reproduced as the first test of 0.8.1.
- **Overlay references that survive paste.** Duplicating a brush and the `info_overlay` on it gives a copy
  whose `sides` still lists the original's faces. In the game the copy draws on the original wall. This is the
  second test of 0.8.1.
- **One predicate for "this side is a displacement".** Four places re-derive it from key lookups
  (`solid_mesh.cpp:85`, `bake_scene.cpp:69`, `bake_casters.cpp:45`, `bsp_lightmaps.cpp:145`). D1 replaces them.
- A displacement mesh, picking on one, a sculpt or paint tool, sewing, overlay or decal drawing, hotspot rects,
  a rope shape, or detail/structural commands.

## 1. Decisions

### D1: A displacement stays a VMF `dispinfo` block; a pure `geometry::Displacement` is parsed from it on demand

The side block remains the truth (0.2 D4). `geometry/displacement.hpp` adds a value type, `Displacement`,
with power, start position, flags, elevation, subdivision, and per-vertex normals, distances, offsets, offset
normals, alphas, triangle tags, allowed vertices and (if D7 finds it) multiblend data. `parseDisplacement` and
`writeDisplacement` convert between it and a `kv1::Node`. Unknown keys and rows inside `dispinfo` are kept in
place, as `OpaqueBlock` does today. `Document` caches the parsed value per `SideId`, refreshed from the
ChangeSet as the side index already is. Every consumer (mirror, picking, baker, VRAD matching, the tools) asks
one function, `document::displacement(SideId)`, which returns an empty optional for a flat side.

The alternative is a first-class `Displacement` node in the document tree with its own ID. That is turned down
because a displacement has no identity apart from its side: Hammer's file has no ID for it, undo, selection
and the outliner already key by `SideId`, and a second object would need its lifetime kept in step with the
side through every rebuild and paste.

Values outside their valid range (alpha outside 0–255, as in test1's displacement) are kept as written, clamped
only where they are drawn or baked, and reported in the Problems list ("displacement alpha out of range").
Apex never rewrites them unless the user paints over them.

Writes go through `Document::setDisplacement(SideId, const Displacement&)`, a single transaction write. It
re-serialises only the rows that changed, so a map whose displacements are not edited saves byte-identical,
and a one-row edit diffs as one row.

*What would reverse it:* a profile from D6's sculpt tool showing that parsing and re-serialising `dispinfo`
per stroke is too slow at power 4 on a 16×16 terrain, even with the cache. The fallback is to keep the parsed
value as the stored form and serialise only on save, which changes the 0.2 D4 contract and is not taken
lightly.

### D2: The displacement mesh is VBSP's, checked vertex by vertex against compiled `DISP_VERTS`

`geometry::displacementMesh(const Face& base, const Displacement&)` returns the (2^p+1)² grid: positions,
per-vertex normals, alphas and the triangle list with its tags. The rules (which base vertex is the start
corner, how rows and columns run, and how normals×distances and offsets combine; elevation and offset normals turned out to be ignored, see below)
follow VBSP, because the compile is what the player sees. Hammer agrees with VBSP on every displacement it
writes, so matching VBSP also matches Hammer's view.

The oracle is bsppp: compile each D3 fixture, read `DISPINFO` and `DISP_VERTS`, and compare. The tolerance is
0.01 units (well above VBSP's float storage error at ±65536; research.md §1). The test fails on the first
vertex outside it, and names the fixture, side and grid index.

The alternative is to port Hammer's `CMapDisp` from memory or from the leaked SDK. That is turned down: it
cannot be checked except against the compile anyway, and Apex is MIT (roadmap principle 6).

*Measured in 0.8.1 (2026-11-10, `tools/disp_oracle.py`, fixture `test1_disp`):*
- `DISP_VERTS` is in VMF row-major order, whatever `startposition` is.
- Each vertex's vector×dist is normal×distance plus offset, with offsets in world space.
- **`elevation` and `offset_normals` are ignored by this VBSP:** variants that change them compile to identical
  `DISP_VERTS`, and neither string is in the binary. Apex's mesh ignores them too, and still preserves and
  transforms them.
- Alphas are copied unclamped as floats (test1's −96 … 72 reach the BSP as written).
- The start corner and the row and column directions are only indirectly confirmed. Apex's rule is: start at
  the base corner nearest `startposition`, with rows running c0→c1 in Source's clockwise corner order. It
  agrees with Hammer's triangle tags and with test1's alpha rows. D3 fixture 1, with each corner as the start,
  settles it.
- **This vbsp segfaults in "Finding displacement neighbors" when a map with a displacement leaks** (exit 139,
  native and win64 under Wine). A sealed map with displacements, test1 included, compiles.

Non-quad base faces: VBSP rejects a displacement on a face with other than 4 vertices. Apex draws such a side
as its flat face, reports it in the Problems list (0.5) with the compiler's wording, and never creates one.

*What would reverse it:* a D3 fixture whose compiled vertices differ from what Strata Hammer draws in its
viewport. Then the plan records both, matches VBSP, and notes the difference.

### D3: The displacement oracle is a fixture set the owner authors in Strata Hammer, plus property tests

Nothing on disk has a displacement (above), so 0.8.1 builds a fixture set:
`tests/fixtures/vmf/disp/*.vmf` and their compiled `.bsp`, authored in Strata Hammer by the owner. It
covers:

1. power 2, 3 and 4 on axis-aligned quads, with each base vertex as the start corner;
2. a rotated and a sheared base face (non-axis-aligned, non-rectangular);
3. elevation, painted alpha and sculpted offsets;
4. a 3×3 sewn patch with mixed powers (2 next to 3), corners and T-edges;
5. a subdivided patch (`subdiv 1`);
6. triangle tags and `NoPhys`/`NoHull`/`NoRay` flags;
7. a WVT material blend;
8. multiblend, if D7 finds that Strata supports it;
9. a displacement inside a `func_instance`, and one in a `func_detail`.

Each fixture is round-tripped (load and save unmodified: byte-identical), meshed (D2 against `DISP_VERTS`),
and opened again in Strata Hammer after an Apex edit (D15's interop check). Property tests run the mesher on
random valid `Displacement` values and check invariants: grid size, edges shared exactly between sewn
neighbours, and transform equivariance (D4).

The alternative is to synthesise displacements with Apex itself and compile them. That is circular for D2 (the
writer and the reader would share any mistake), so Apex-written files only join the corpus after the
Hammer-authored ones pass.

The cost is the owner's time in Strata Hammer, about a dozen small maps. It is stated now because 0.8.2 cannot
merge without them.

*What would reverse it:* a public, licence-compatible set of Strata or Source maps with displacements and their
compiled BSPs (for example community P2CE maps whose authors agree). Those would replace or extend the set.

### D4: Every transform carries the displacement with it, through one function

`geometry::transformDisplacement(Displacement&, const Transform&, const Face& newBase)` replaces the code in
instance collapse and is called by `applyToSolid`, paste-with-offset, paste special, instance collapse and
prefab placement. For a linear map A with translation t, each grid point p becomes A·p + t exactly:
`startposition` is transformed, each normal n becomes A·n/|A·n| with its distance scaled by |A·n|, and offsets
and offset normals are multiplied by A. Because the base grid is a bilinear blend of the corners, and that
commutes with affine maps, the result is exact for translation, rotation, scale and shear.

Mirroring reverses the base face's winding, so the grid's row/column order flips. The function reindexes the
grid so that the mesh is the mirror image and not a mirror image turned inside out. A property test checks
`mesh(transform(d)) == transform(mesh(d))` for random affine maps, mirrors included.

The alternative is Hammer's behaviour on scale, which Valve documents only as "displacements may distort".
Exact equivariance costs nothing extra here, and is turned down only if D3's interop check shows Hammer reading
the scaled result differently.

*What would reverse it:* nothing short of a VBSP constraint (for example a cap on distance) that the scaled
values break. Then scale clamps and warns.

### D5: Topology tools act on displaced solids by rule, and the rule is per tool

0.3 D11's blanket refusal is replaced:

| Tool | On a solid with a displacement side |
|---|---|
| Transforms, nudge, paste offset | Carry the displacement (D4). |
| Texture tools, Face Properties, apply | Edit the side as now; `dispinfo` is untouched. |
| Vertex tool | Refuses on the displaced side's vertices; allowed on the solid's other vertices only when the displaced face keeps its 4 vertices and its plane changes affinely (the mesh is then re-based via D4). |
| Clip | Refuses when the plane cuts a displaced face. Allowed when it removes only flat sides. |
| Carve, hollow, merge | Refuse, with a message, as now. |
| Destroy displacement (new command) | Turns the side flat again, one transaction, undoable. |

The alternative is Hammer's: operate, and silently drop the displacement. It stays turned down for the same
reason as in 0.3 (silent data loss on one click). A user who wants Hammer's result runs *Destroy displacement*
first, which says what it does.

The cost: Hammer users expect clip to cut a terrain patch. Re-sampling a clipped displacement onto a new grid
is possible but lossy and rarely wanted. It is listed in §4.

*What would reverse it:* the owner's playtest showing the refusals block a common outdoor workflow, most
likely clipping a terrain block's flat underside, which this rule already allows.

### D6: One Displacement tool with modes, and a sculpt stroke is one transaction

The tool is one `editor::Tool` (0.3 D1) with modes in its readout: **Create** (pick faces; set power,
elevation; quad faces only), **Sew**, **Sculpt**, **Paint alpha**, and **Paint multiblend** if D7 allows it.
(Subdivide was built in 0.8.3 and dropped on 2026-11-14: Hammer's Subdivide only smooths, which the Smooth
brush does under the user's control, and power changes are Face Properties' job.) Sculpt brushes are Raise/Lower, Smooth, Flatten to plane, Set height and Noise. They take a radius,
a falloff curve and a strength, act along the vertex normal, the face normal or a chosen axis, and work in any
viewport, orthographic included (principle 1: the tool sees a ray).

A stroke from press to release is previewed on a live mesh owned by the tool (0.3 D12's preview), and
committed once at release as one `setDisplacement` per touched side. Undo then takes back a stroke, as in
Hammer++.

The alternative, a transaction per mouse-move sample, makes undo useless (hundreds of steps per stroke) and
re-serialises `dispinfo` many times a second (D1's reversal condition).

Sew is a command, not automatic: it snaps shared edge and corner vertices of the selected displacements to
their average, as Hammer does. The Problems list (0.5) gains "unsewn displacement edge": two displacements
sharing a base edge whose vertices differ by more than 0.1 units.

*What would reverse it:* the owner preferring per-dab undo (rare in sculpt tools), or Sew needing to run after
every sculpt stroke to avoid cracks, in which case Sculpt sews the stroke's border as part of its commit.

### D7: Multiblend is gated on a P0 check of Strata's shaders; WVT alpha blending is built either way

Hammer's alpha painting drives WorldVertexTransition, which Strata's LightmappedGeneric and PBR shaders
support. Multiblend (four layers, `multiblend` and `alphablend` rows and `multiblend_color_*`) came from CS:GO.
0.8.1's P0 checks whether Strata's shipped shaders and VBSP read it: a probe map with a multiblend `dispinfo`
and a `Lightmapped_4WayBlend` or `Multiblend` material, compiled and run.

- If the game draws it, 0.8.4 builds multiblend painting and Realtime preview.
- If not, multiblend rows are preserved (D1), shown in the displacement panel as read-only, and the release
  notes say so.

Both outcomes match the roadmap ("paint alpha/multiblend"), because the roadmap asks for painting what the game
draws.

*Evidence so far (2026-11-10):* P2CE ships `materials/grass/hr_grass/4wb_grass_a.vmt`, a `Lightmapped_4WayBlend`
material with four base textures. The first outcome is therefore likely, but the probe still decides it. The game
also ships 18 `WorldVertexTransition` materials in Portal 2's VPK (for example `nature/blendgrassdirt01_noprop`,
with a `$blendmodulatetexture`, and `nature/blend_grass_gravel_01`). These are D3 fixture 7's candidates.

*What would reverse it:* the probe showing partial support, for example VBSP keeps the rows but the shader
ignores them. Then it is preserved only, as in the second outcome.

### D8: Displacements draw in every display mode, with WVT and blend-modulate shading in Realtime

The render mirror meshes displacement sides through D2 and draws them in Wireframe, Flat, Textured and Realtime.
Each vertex carries its alpha, so the 0.6.5 deferred work, WorldVertexTransition `$basetexture2`,
`$blendmodulatetexture` and the PBR blend layer, can now be built and checked against in-game captures as 0.6
D2 did.

Picking hits the triangle mesh (reusing 0.4's model-triangle picking), and face selection still selects the
side by `SideId`. Snapping gains displacement vertices (the snapper's vertex priority, 0.3). The shadow casters
and the BVH take the displacement mesh if 0.8.5's P0 finds that VRAD lets displacements cast (0.7 D1's rule:
only what reaches the compile casts, and only as the compile does).

*What would reverse it:* nothing expected. The shading is checked against in-game captures, and any material
that cannot match is recorded as 0.6 recorded parallax.

### D9: Displacement lightmaps are charted, baked and matched to VRAD in 0.8

0.7 §3 handed displacement lightmaps forward. VBSP charts a displaced face over its base face's lightmap
extents without the 128-luxel subdivision (to be confirmed against bsppp in P0), and samples luxels at
positions on the displaced surface (`DISP_LIGHTMAP_SAMPLE_POSITIONS`). 0.8.5:

- extends `lighting::chartFace` with a displacement path, checked face by face against the D3 compiles;
- bakes displaced luxels on the GPU and CPU backends;
- maps VRAD's displacement lightmaps back to sides by geometry (0.7 D5 already counts and skips them,
  `bsp_lightmaps.cpp:168`);
- lets Hybrid rebake displacements an edit touched.

The alternative is to show displacements unlit (fullbright or Realtime only) in Baked, VRAD and Hybrid until a
later milestone. That is cheaper, but an outdoor map is mostly displacement, so those views would be mostly
wrong on exactly the maps 0.8 is for.

*What would reverse it:* the P0 showing that VRAD's displacement sample positions cannot be recovered by
geometry from the BSP (for example if they depend on an internal neighbour table). Then the VRAD view samples
by nearest luxel position and accepts a stated error.

### D10: Overlays are clipped to their faces on the CPU, the way VBSP does

An `info_overlay` is a quad defined by `BasisOrigin`, `BasisU`, `BasisV`, `BasisNormal`, `uv0`–`uv3` and
`StartU/StartV/EndU/EndV`, projected onto the faces in its `sides` list. VBSP clips the projected quad against
each listed face and writes the fragments. Apex does the same in `geometry/overlay.cpp`: pure, clipped against
each face polygon (the displaced mesh, if the face is a displacement), and returned as fragments with UVs.
The mirror draws fragments with the overlay's material, depth bias, and in Realtime, lit like the face under it
(0.6 §4).

The alternative is a deferred projected decal in the fragment shader. It needs a G-buffer Apex does not have
(0.6 D4 is forward+), and it would draw outside the listed faces, which VBSP never does. CPU clipping matches
the compile, and overlays are few (the corpus maximum is checked in 0.8.6's P0).

`info_overlay_transition` (water shorelines) uses the same clip, with its two side lists. `infodecal` is placed
by the engine at load, not by VBSP: it projects onto the nearest brush surface within the decal's extent along
the surface normal. D12 previews it as an approximation and says so in its tooltip.

*What would reverse it:* a corpus map with thousands of overlays where CPU clipping on edit is slow. Then
fragments are cached per overlay and rebuilt only when its listed faces change (the ChangeSet already says
which).

### D11: Overlay side lists follow their faces through every edit, in the same transaction

`sides` is an FGD `sidelist` key, so the rule covers any entity with such a key, not only overlays.

1. **Paste, duplicate, paste special, prefab and instance collapse** remap `sidelist` keys through the pasted
   side map, in one function (the one in `instance_collapse_command.cpp`, moved to `document/`). This fixes the
   latent paste bug. A listed face that was not copied stays pointing at the original face, as Hammer does when
   only the overlay is copied.
2. **Faces that vanish** (`vanishedSides`) are removed from every `sidelist` that lists them in the same
   transaction, so undo restores both together.
3. **A rebuild that splits a face** (clip, carve) adds the new fragment IDs to any list that held the parent,
   using the face-matching correspondence (0.3 D2).
4. **An overlay left with no faces** is kept and flagged in the Problems list ("overlay has no faces", select
   and open). VBSP drops such an overlay with a warning.

The alternatives are to leave stale IDs and only warn (Hammer's behaviour in some paths, which leaves the
file wrong until someone fixes it by hand), or to delete overlays automatically when their last face goes (a
surprise in a long session). Both are turned down.

*What would reverse it:* a case where remapping on split attaches an overlay to a fragment it does not cover.
Rule 3 then filters the added fragments by the overlay's projected footprint.

### D12: An Overlay tool places on a face, and the footprint chooses the faces

The Overlay tool (0.3 D1 interface) places an `info_overlay` with the active material on the face under the
cursor, oriented to the face's texture axes. It sizes from the material's aspect ratio. Its `sides` list is
every face the projected quad touches within 45° of the basis normal, not only the clicked face. Shift-click
adds or removes faces from the list by hand, and Face Properties gains an "Overlays on this face" list.

Handles move, rotate and scale the quad and drag its four UV corners in any viewport. Decals are placed by the
same tool in a Decal mode, as an `infodecal` with `texture` set, previewed by D10's approximation.

Hammer adds only the clicked face, which is the usual cause of overlays cut off at a brush edge. Auto-adding
by footprint fixes the common case, and the explicit list keeps the result predictable and visible.

*What would reverse it:* the owner finding the automatic list adds faces they did not want, for example the
far side of a thin wall. Then the 45° limit tightens, or auto-add becomes a modifier.

### D13: Hotspot rects come from the VTF resource first, then a `.rect` file; fitting is pure

`assets::HotspotSet` is loaded per material: first the VTF's hotspot resource (vtfpp `HOT`), then a `.rect`
file named by the VMT or found next to it (Hammer++'s text format). The first found wins and is shown in the
material browser as a badge.

`geometry::fitHotspot(face, rects, options)` chooses the rect whose aspect and size best match the face in
texels, and writes `uaxis`/`vaxis`/scale/shift so the face fills it, respecting the rect flags (rotation
allowed, reflection allowed, random among equals). It is pure and tested with fixed rect sets.

The UI is a Hotspot button in Face Properties, and a Hotspot mode of the apply tool that fits on each click.
Randomness uses a seed stored per click, so redo reproduces it.

0.8.7's P0 looks for hotspot data in the P2CE VPKs (none was found loose). If none ships, the fixtures are a
hand-made VTF with a `HOT` resource and a `.rect` file.

*What would reverse it:* Strata documenting a third source (for example a VMT key naming a rect map) or a
different precedence. Then `HotspotSet` follows Strata.

### D14: Ropes are previewed by the engine's rope rule, settled offline

`keyframe_rope` and `move_rope` chains (linked by `NextKey`) already draw as straight polylines (0.4). 0.8
draws each segment with sag. The shape comes from the same relaxation the engine runs when a rope spawns:
`Slack` lengthens the rest length, `Type` sets the point count (corrected in 0.8.8: `Subdiv` only smooths the
drawn curve), and gravity settles it. It runs to rest on the
CPU after each edit, and draws as a tube of `Width` with `RopeMaterial`.

The alternative is a fitted catenary through the two ends with length = distance + `Slack`. It is simpler, but
the engine is not a catenary at low `Subdiv`, and the difference shows on short slack ropes.

The preview is checked against in-game screenshots of the 4 corpus maps' ropes, and of a probe map with
`Slack` 0, 25, 100 and 400 and `Subdiv` 2 and 8. A Rope tool clicks out a chain, creates the entities and links
`NextKey`.

*What would reverse it:* the in-game check showing the relaxation disagrees no better than a catenary. Then
the simpler catenary is used.

### D15: The exit is a scripted outdoor scene built in Apex, opened unchanged in Strata Hammer, and played

"Parity with Hammer++" needs a test that can fail. The 0.8 release runs a written scenario on Linux:

1. build an outdoor area: a 4×4 sewn power-3 terrain with a sculpted hill and a valley, a WVT grass/rock blend
   painted by alpha, a road overlay crossing three faces, two decals, a sagging cable between two poles, and a
   hotspot-textured wall;
2. save, open in Strata Hammer, and save again from Hammer without edits: Apex reloads that file and its
   displacements, overlays and ropes are equal to what Apex wrote (same mesh, same `sides`, same keys);
3. compile and play: the in-game scene matches Apex's Realtime view by the owner's eye, and the D2 mesh test
   passes on the compiled BSP;
4. the same scene's Baked and Hybrid views against VRAD, with the 0.7 parity statistics recorded, not gated.

The alternative is a feature checklist against Hammer++'s documentation. It is easier to tick but does not show
the workflow end to end, and Hammer++ is not available on this platform to compare side by side.

*What would reverse it:* the owner preferring a different reference scene (for example an existing
community map).

### D16: Detail/structural tooling is commands and a view, not a new geometry type

"Detail/structural tooling" covers:

- **To Detail / To World**: one-step `func_detail` versions of the existing *Tie to entity* and *Move to
  world* (`EditorSession::tieToEntity`, `moveToWorld`), bound in the pie menu;
- a **Structural** display filter that greys out detail brushes and entities so the vis-relevant shell
  stands out, and a matching outliner filter;
- a **Problems** check for structural brushes that are small or thin enough to fragment vis (thresholds from
  the Valve developer wiki's guidance, confirmed against `.prt` portal counts on the corpus);
- **detail props**: the `detail.vbsp` sprites and models that VBSP scatters on displacements whose material
  sets `%detailtype`. They are previewed as instanced quads and models, using VBSP's placement rule, which is
  confirmed against compiled `sprp`/detail lumps in P0.

The alternative reading, a vis-cluster visualiser, overlaps 0.5's `.prt` overlay and is not repeated.

*What would reverse it:* VBSP's detail-prop placement proving impractical to reproduce from the compiled lumps
(0.8.9 P0). Then detail props drop to a later milestone and the commands, filter and check ship alone. The
owner confirmed this scope on 2026-11-10.

### 0.8.2: The displacement core and read-only display

**Work.** D1 value type, parse/write and document cache; the single displacement predicate replacing the four
call sites; D2 mesh; D8 drawing in all modes, picking, snapping; the Problems entry for non-quad bases; the
displacement panel showing power, flags and elevation (read-only).

**As built.**
- One predicate, `document::isDisplaced(side)` / `displacedSides(solid)`, replaces the hand-rolled checks in
  render, picking, lighting and topology.
- Texture UVs are the side's projection at the flat (undisplaced) grid point. That is how VBSP and the engine
  interpolate the base face's corner texcoords, so cliffs stretch as they do in game.
- Normals are smooth and area-weighted. `BrushVertex` gains `alpha` (48 bytes), clamped to 0..255 and divided
  by 255. It is not yet a vertex attribute; 0.8.4 binds it at location 5.
- Wireframe draws every triangle edge.
- A dispinfo that does not parse, or sits on a non-quad base, draws flat and is reported in Problems
  (`DisplacementUnreadable`, `DisplacementNonQuad`). Out-of-range alphas raise `DisplacementAlphaRange`.
- Each `BatchMesh` is indexed as [displaced | flat | displaced sides' flat base faces]. Draws use the first two
  ranges; shadow casters and the scene BVH use the last two. A displacement still casts as its flat base face
  until 0.8.5's probe decides (D8).
- No displacement lightmaps yet. Baked mode keeps the Lit path for displaced faces.
- Picking, marquee, bounds and Surface snap use the displaced mesh. Grid vertices snap as
  `DisplacementVertex`. The vertex tool ignores them.
- The face panel shows power, flags and elevation, read-only. Flag names assume 2 = NoPhys, 4 = NoHull and
  8 = NoRay, from memory. D3 fixture 6 must confirm them.
- Known limit: a vertex or clip preview of a displaced solid meshes flat (preview values carry no dispinfo).
- A displacement brush's other sides do not exist in game: no face, no collision, and Hammer hides them (the
  owner confirmed). `document::hiddenByDisplacement` is the one rule. Those sides are not drawn in any mode,
  cast no shadow, are not picked, marqueed or snapped to, and are not baked. A solid's pick bounds are its
  displaced surfaces. The rule keys on `isDisplaced`, so an unreadable displacement still hides its siblings
  and draws its own side flat.
- Open for 0.8.3: the vertex and clip tools still edit the hidden box. D5 decides what each tool may do.

### 0.8.7: Hotspot texturing

**P0.** Hotspot data in the P2CE VPKs; Hammer++'s `.rect` format against a sample file.
**Work.** D13 `HotspotSet`, `fitHotspot`, the button, the apply mode, the browser badge.
**Tests.** Fitting against fixed rect sets: aspect choice, rotation and reflection flags, seeded randomness;
redo reproduces a random fit.

**As built.**
- P0 (2026-11-14): none of P2CE's 5,665 VTFs (3,829 in VPKs, 1,836 loose) carries a hotspot resource. No
  `.rect` file ships, and no VMT has a `%rectanglemap` key. The fixtures are hand-made: a VTF 7.3 with the
  resource, built in the test, and `.rect` text.
- Formats follow the Strata wiki ("Hotspot Embedded Format", "Hotspot Text Format"), read from the
  StrataSource/Wiki repository:
  - The resource tag is `+\0\0` (0x2B). Its body is u8 version (1), u8 flags, u16 count, then
    {u8 flags, u16 min x, min y, max x, max y} per rect, in pixels from the top left.
  - Rect flags are rotate 0x1, reflect 0x2 and alt 0x4.
  - The `.rect` format is `Rectangles { rectangle { min "x y" max "x y" rotate 1 reflect 1 alt 1 } }`.
    The VMT names it with `%rectanglemap`.
- `assets::loadHotspots` checks the sources in this order:
  1. The `$basetexture` VTF's resource. The first 64 KiB are read, and the whole file only when the resource
     runs past them.
  2. The `%rectanglemap` file.
  3. `materials/<material>.rect`.

  A broken file is an error naming its path, not a silent fall-through. `GameContent::hotspots` caches the
  result per material, and a reload clears the cache.
- `geometry::HotspotRect` duplicates `assets::HotspotRegion`, because geometry may not depend on assets. ui
  converts between them for the session's `faceHotspots` hook.
- The fit rule is ours, because Strata does not publish its scoring:
  - The frame is the current U axis projected into the face.
  - The score is 2·|ln aspect ratio| + |ln size ratio|, with the size taken at the default texture scale.
  - A rect without the rotate flag may turn a quarter only to beat its unturned fit by more than 1e-3, as a
    trim strip running up a pillar.
  - Candidates within 1e-6 of the best are equals, and a splitmix64 seed picks among them, along with the
    reflect flip.
  - The face then fills the rect exactly, with a separate scale on each axis.
- Alt-group rects are used only with Alt (Strata's left-Alt rule), and then exclusively.
- Each click draws a new seed. The fitted axes are committed, so redo replays the same result without
  storing the seed.
- Face Properties has a Hotspot button, which Alt+click switches to the alt group. It commits "Hotspot faces",
  skips faces whose material has no rects, and is disabled when none has any.
- The Apply tool has a Mode parameter (Material or Hotspot). In Hotspot mode, an apply click sets the
  material and fits the face as one "Apply hotspot" step. Alt picks the alt group.
- In the material browser, a thumbnail is tagged "H" when its VTF has the resource (the thumbnail job, which
  already reads the file, checks it), or when a `.rect` exists. Known limit: the job reads the preview texture,
  which may be `%tooltexture` rather than `$basetexture`, so a resource on the base texture alone can go
  unbadged. Fitting is not affected.

### 0.8.8: Ropes and cables

**P0.** In-game captures of the rope probe map (D14).
**Work.** Rope relaxation and tube drawing; Rope tool.
**Tests.** Relaxation is deterministic; shapes within a stated tolerance of the captures.

**As built.**
- The FGD fix came first (2026-11-14). Apex took only `@PointClass` as a point entity, so P2CE's ropes, which
  are `@MoveClass`, could not be placed. `assets::isPointClass` now also accepts NPCClass, KeyFrameClass,
  MoveClass and FilterClass. The real-FGD test checks move_rope, keyframe_rope, npc_headcrab_black and
  filter_multi.
- The rule was read from Source SDK 2013 (rope.cpp, c_rope.cpp, rope_physics.cpp, simple_physics.cpp,
  rope_shared.h), and `geometry/rope.hpp` sets it out. Two points correct D14:
  - `Type` sets the node count: 0 gives 10, 1 gives 4, anything else 2, and an entity with no Type key gets 5.
  - `Subdiv` only adds Catmull-Rom points between nodes when drawing. 255 means the default of 2, and the
    maximum is 7.
- The constants are:
  - The rest length is (int distance + Slack − 100) / (nodes − 1), at least 0.
  - Verlet steps of 1/50 s, with gravity −1500 and 0.98 of the previous motion kept.
  - 3 constraint passes per step that only pull neighbours together, re-locking the ends after each pass.
    Dangling unlocks the far end.
- The engine hangs the rope for 5 s at spawn and keeps simulating. `ropeNodes` runs the 5 s, then carries on
  until no node moves 1e-6 in a step (capped at 5 min). A rope takes 0.1–0.4 ms in clang-release.
- Two engine artefacts are kept, and the tests allow for them:
  - A tight rope leans toward its start, because the constraint sweep runs from start to end.
  - Gravity stretches a dangling rope about 1% past its rest length.
- Each keyframe draws its rope to each NextKey peer as a helper polyline, once per entity (the class has both
  the keyframe and animator helpers). A move preview re-settles a rope whose ends move apart, and shifts it
  when both move together.
- Duplicate (not Paste) strings keyframe chains; `editor/keyframe_chain` has the rules.
  - Duplicating one keyframe inserts the copy after it: the original points at the copy, and the copy points at
    the original's old NextKey. An unnamed original is named `<classname>_1` first, and the copy gets the next
    free number with its zero padding kept.
  - Duplicating several renames each copy the same way. Copies that pointed at a copied original are re-aimed
    at its copy, so the copies form a chain of their own.
- Tie to Entity: a new brush entity with no `origin` gets the centre of its solids' bounds.
- Not done:
  - The tube drawn with `Width` and `RopeMaterial`. Ropes are lines for now.
  - The Rope tool.
  - The P0 in-game captures. The shape is checked against the SDK's rule, not against a capture yet.
  - Wind and `Collide` are left out of the preview on purpose.

## 5. Things that will bite

**1. Someone tests the mesh against Apex's own writer.** It is tempting once Create works, because it is easy to
generate cases. A writer bug then passes every test. *Defence:* D3's Hammer-authored fixtures are the oracle
for D2. Apex-written files join only after those pass.

**2. A new transform path forgets the displacement.** Paste special, prefabs, instance placement and modal
transforms each build their own transform today. Only `applyToSolid` (`transform.cpp`) and instance collapse
move geometry by a transform today; the clipboard places pasted blocks by its own route. *Defence:*
`transformDisplacement` is called from `applyToSolid` and from every paste and placement path, and the 0.8.1
test runs every transform and paste action in `actions.cpp` on a displaced solid.

**3. Mirroring turns a displacement inside out.** A mirror reverses winding, so the start corner and the row
order both change. *Defence:* the equivariance property test includes negative-determinant maps.

**4. A paste path forgets to remap side lists.** It is easy to add a new way of creating copies. *Defence:*
the clipboard's paste (`clipboard.cpp`, which already records the side map) applies the remap itself before
it returns; no caller does it.

**5. Re-serialising `dispinfo` on every mouse move.** It looks harmless at power 2 and stalls at power 4 on a
large terrain. *Defence:* D6 commits once per stroke, and 0.8.4 has a timing test on a 16×16 power-4 terrain.

**6. Sewn neighbours crack after a stroke.** A stroke that ends at a shared edge moves one side's border
vertices and not the other's. *Defence:* the stroke moves shared vertices on both sides (they are found by the
same edge matching Sew uses), and the unsewn-edge check runs in the stroke's test.

**7. Overlay fragments disagree with the compile at a T-junction.** VBSP clips against the BSP face fragments,
not the brush faces. *Defence:* 0.8.6 compares fragments against the compiled overlay lump on all 25 corpus
maps, not only fixtures.

**8. A leak in a map with displacements shows up as a vbsp crash, not a leak.** VBSP segfaults before it writes
the `.lin` file, so 0.5's leak overlay has nothing to show and the log ends mid-step. *Defence:* 0.8.3 adds a
compile diagnostic. When vbsp exits on a signal during "Finding displacement neighbors", the Problems list says
the map probably leaks, and suggests compiling once with the displacements' solids hidden (or cordoned) to find
the leak.

**9. The decal preview is mistaken for the compile.** `infodecal` is placed by the engine, not VBSP.
*Defence:* the tooltip and the panel say "approximate".
